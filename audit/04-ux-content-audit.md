# Phase 4 - UX/UI and Content Hierarchy Audit

Evidence sources:

- Baseline live crawl
- Baseline Lighthouse
- Visual/manual review of the live site
- Repository content and assets

## Strategic Questions

| Question | Baseline Assessment |
|---|---|
| Can a first-time visitor tell what the business does within seconds? | Partly. The eyebrow and metadata mention personal training, but the H1 is abstract. |
| Is it explicit that this is fitness coaching/personal training? | Partly. Present in hero eyebrow and title, but not the headline. |
| Are concrete outcomes clear? | Not early enough. Weight loss is present, but getting fitter/building strength/getting back in shape are not strongly surfaced. |
| Is personal training obvious? | Somewhat. It appears in service copy and title. |
| Is online coaching obvious? | Present but not strongly prioritised. |
| Is weight loss mentioned naturally? | Yes, but it could be more prominent. |
| Is getting fitter mentioned naturally? | Weak in baseline. |
| Is building strength mentioned naturally? | Weak in baseline. |
| Is getting back in shape mentioned naturally? | Weak in baseline. |
| Does the visitor recognise themselves? | Yes, but the language leans philosophical before practical. |
| Is proof visible early enough? | No genuine proof/testimonials are available in the repo; baseline uses “feedback” cards rather than verified proof. |
| Are services easy to understand? | The service cards explain help areas, but purchasable routes could be clearer. |
| Is Phil visible and credible as a human coach? | Yes, but Phil appears after abstract positioning. |
| Is the enquiry path obvious? | Yes, but CTA wording is more strategy-call oriented than fitness-outcome oriented. |
| Does the philosophy differentiate rather than obscure? | Baseline philosophy is distinctive but appears too early in the hierarchy. |

## Baseline Strengths

- Strong, premium visual identity.
- Distinctive Rebirth/Areté concept.
- Good typography and restrained palette.
- Simple single-page flow.
- Functional same-page navigation.
- Clear enquiry form.
- Phil appears as a real human coach.

## Baseline Weaknesses

- Hero H1 is emotionally interesting but not commercially clear.
- Concrete fitness outcomes are not immediate enough.
- Services are not framed as clear “what you can buy/enquire about” options.
- Proof is not supported by genuine testimonials or transformation content in the repository.
- Orange eyebrow labels fail contrast on light backgrounds.
- Placeholder contact/review/canonical details reduce trust if they are visible in production.

## Recommended Homepage Hierarchy

1. Clear fitness outcome.
2. “Is this you?” problem recognition.
3. Practical progress/proof framework, without fabricated claims.
4. Services: 1-to-1 PT, online coaching, nutrition/accountability.
5. Why Rebirth Areté is different.
6. About Phil.
7. Final CTA/enquiry form.

## Brand Preservation Guidance

Preserve:

- Slate/orange/soft palette.
- Rebirth Areté logo treatment.
- Playfair Display + Inter typography.
- Anti-restart positioning.
- Philosophy around rebuilding, standards, and sustainable change.
- Calm, premium, human tone.

Avoid:

- Generic gym-bro aggression.
- Fake urgency.
- Red/black fitness template styling.
- Fabricated transformations or testimonials.
- Keyword-stuffed local/location claims.

## UX Priority Summary

1. Make the hero outcome-led.
2. Put problem recognition immediately below the hero.
3. Make service options scannable and actionable.
4. Move the philosophy later as differentiation.
5. Keep Phil visible and credible.
6. Use the final form as the clear conversion endpoint.
